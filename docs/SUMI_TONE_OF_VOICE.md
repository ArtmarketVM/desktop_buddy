# sumi tone of voice

## Use active language about what the user did

Owner decision: 21 October 2026. Applies to chats, mascot messages, progress summaries and activity descriptions.

Describe the user's concrete action in the active voice. Address them as "you", use a specific verb and name the activity. Include the duration when relevant. Talk about the work itself rather than the app's bookkeeping: avoid formulas such as "18 minutes of customer emails recorded", "time was recorded" or "activity has been logged".

Prefer: "You spent 18 minutes emailing customers."

When sending is confirmed: "You sent emails to customers over 18 minutes."

When only drafting is observed: "You spent 18 minutes drafting customer emails."

Keep the evidence accurate: time spent in an email app does not by itself prove that emails were sent. Active wording must not invent a completed result.

Other examples:

- "You finished the deck outline." rather than "Deck outline completion recorded."
- "You submitted the VAT return." rather than "The VAT return was recorded as complete."
- "Here’s what you did today" rather than "Your day, recorded".

When sumi itself performs an action, name that action directly: "I’ve added the pitch deck to Today." This describes what happened without passive bookkeeping language.

The shared coaching voice guide and mascot notification templates apply this rule. Keep the existing calm-colleague voice: plain words, no praise, no exclamation marks, no invented outcomes or deadlines.

## Questions and buttons

The main buttons answer the message's main question. Chat is always available as a secondary action, never the accent action. A deadline asks whether the user is on it or needs to reschedule; its buttons are "I’m on it", "Reschedule" and secondary "Chat". A direct request in chat to add a goal or step is already authorization and does not need another confirmation.
